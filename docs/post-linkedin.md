I built a 3D game for my kids. Then they started finding the bugs I couldn't.

**UnicornRush** is a running game for children aged 3 to 7. Twenty-two
unicorns, fifteen tracks, and no 3D assets anywhere — every unicorn, tree,
cloud and lollipop is drawn in code with three.js.

A few things I'm glad I got right:

🔑 **Everything is earned by running.** Magic keys are the only currency.
Every unicorn and every track is unlocked by playing. Nothing is bought.

📖 **There's a story, and it has an ending.** An illustrated storybook
explains why Uni is running: her friends are locked behind doors. The last
two pages only appear the day all 21 friends are free — and they answer who
locked them away, and why. (He wasn't being cruel. He was invisible.)

🔊 **The game reads itself out loud.** Most of its players can't read yet.
Every unicorn and track card has a listen button, and instead of grabbing the
first voice it finds, the game ranks the ones installed on the device and
picks the best — the default Portuguese voice is usually the worst one there.

🌍 **Two languages**, chosen on first launch: Portuguese and English. 376
phrases, including every unicorn's name, personality and backstory.

📴 **It works offline.** A PWA you install once and that runs on a plane, in
the car, in a waiting room.

The most useful thing wasn't a framework. It was watching a 7-year-old play
it well, a 3-year-old play the obstacle-free mode, and both of them find, in
minutes, the things I'd been guessing about for weeks.

👉 **Want to let your kids have a go?** It's free, runs in any browser and
installs like an app — no store, no account, no ads:
https://unicorn-rush-ten.vercel.app

And if something breaks, or a four-year-old gets stuck somewhere I didn't
plan for, please tell me. Every good fix in this project started with a child
doing something I hadn't thought of — I'd rather hear it than guess again.
